## Introduction: Our Pricing Philosophy - Structure with Strategy

This playbook merges WiBUZ's structured, tier-based pricing with a sophisticated, value-first sales philosophy. It is designed to equip our team with the strategy and tactics required to move beyond transactional sales and into strategic partnerships. Our goal is not merely to sell software licenses; it is to sell **market access, strategic capability, and a definitive competitive advantage**, all framed by our core narrative: **"The Android Moment for the Edge."**

Our guiding principle is simple yet powerful: **We use a scalable pricing architecture, but we execute the sale by pricing the client, not the job.** Our tiers—Foundation, Strategic, United Edge, and our OEM/ISV licenses—are not a simple menu of features. They represent distinct levels of value and partnership. Your mission as a sales professional is to guide clients to the appropriate tier not by listing features, but through a deep, consultative understanding of their unique business objectives, their desired future state, and the economic impact of achieving it.

Mastering this value-based approach is what separates WiBUZ partners from mere vendors. It is how we earn the right to command premium pricing for the transformative outcomes we deliver. This playbook is your guide to executing that mission with precision and confidence.

## 1.0 Core Principles of Value-Based Pricing

Before diving into segment-specific tactics, every member of the sales team must master three foundational principles of value-based pricing. These principles are the bedrock of our sales motion. They shift the conversation away from our costs and deliverables and refocus it squarely on the client's gains and outcomes. Internalizing these concepts will empower you to lead strategic business discussions, not just product demos.

### 1.1 Principle 1: Price the Client, Not the Job

At its core, this principle recognizes that **all value is subjective**. The economic impact of our platform is not determined by the lines of code we write or the hours we spend; it is determined by the scale and strategic importance of the problem we solve for a specific client.

This concept is best illustrated with an analogy. Imagine being asked to generate high-quality inbound leads. The "job" is the same, but the "client" changes everything.

**Client A:** Buff Whelen Chevrolet, a local car dealership. The value of a hundred new leads might translate to a few extra car sales per month—a significant but localized gain.

**Client B:** The Chevrolet division of General Motors. The value of the same percentage increase in leads could translate to an eight or nine-figure impact on their national bottom line.

The work might be similar, but the value is orders of magnitude different. This applies directly to WiBUZ. Delivering multi-vendor support to a 5-person MSP like MsTECH has a specific, high-impact value proposition. Providing a white-label cloud OS to a multi-billion dollar hardware OEM like Fortinet creates an entirely different, exponentially larger value equation—even if some of the underlying technology is shared.

**Our pricing must always reflect the client's unique context and the scale of their opportunity.**

### 1.2 Principle 2: The Value Triad - Quantifying the Client's Gain

Every benefit our platform delivers can be categorized into one of three buckets:

1. **Revenue Gains:** Helping the client make more money, either through top-line sales or bottom-line profit
2. **Cost Reductions:** Helping the client save money or avoid future expenses
3. **Emotional Contributions:** Providing intangible but highly valuable benefits like peace of mind, reduced risk, or enhanced prestige

A successful value conversation uncovers and quantifies all three. While the first two are economic, the third is a powerful magnifier that often drives the final decision and justifies a premium price.

### 1.3 Principle 3: Always Offer Options & Anchor High

Human beings cannot perceive absolute value in a vacuum; we require context and comparisons. Our brains are not wired to ask, **"Is this single price fair?"** They are wired to ask, **"Which of these is the best deal?"** Providing a single price forces the client to seek their own comparisons—usually from our competitors. We must control the comparison.

The WiBUZ pricing tiers are our pre-built "options." Your job is to frame our Foundation, Strategic, and United Edge tiers (or our segment-specific licenses) as three viable paths to achieve the client's desired future state.

The highest tier in any proposal serves as the crucial **"high anchor."** As pricing psychology shows:

> "The third option is not there to be sold—it's there to make the second option look affordable."

When we present the United Edge tier, we are not just selling a product; we are reframing the value of the Strategic tier. With these foundational principles understood, the next step is to execute the sales conversation that uncovers this value and maps it to our solutions.

## 2.0 Executing the Value Conversation: A Three-Step Framework

The **Value Conversation** is not a step in the process; it is the process. This is the critical phase where we seize control of the narrative, prove we are strategic partners, and make a low-ball offer from a competitor seem irrelevant. It is where we transition from a generic pitch to a collaborative diagnosis of the client's business, and it is how we make a premium price feel not just justified, but logical. This three-step framework will guide you through that process.

### 2.1 Step 1: Uncover the Desired Future State

Move the conversation beyond technical needs and product features. Your goal is to understand the client's ultimate business objectives. Ask powerful, open-ended questions that force them to articulate what success truly looks like.

**For OEMs:**
- "Imagine it's 18 months from now and your cloud strategy has failed. What is the most likely reason?"
- "What does success look like, beyond just launching a product?"

**For ISVs:**
- "Walk me through the last major deal you lost. What capability did the winner have that you were missing?"
- "If you could support any hardware vendor tomorrow, which new markets would you enter first?"

**For MSPs:**
- "Describe the type of multi-million dollar deal you feel you should be winning but can't currently compete for. What's the single biggest capability gap holding you back?"

### 2.2 Step 2: Quantify the Value & The Cost of Inaction

Once a desired future state is established, the next step is to attach economic figures to it. Use the ROI anchors from our GTM strategy to frame the value and highlight the significant cost of maintaining the status quo. This transforms a "nice-to-have" solution into a "must-have" business investment.

### 2.3 Step 3: Map the Discovered Value to WiBUZ Tiers

This final step connects the client's specific, quantified problems to our structured solutions. This is the bridge from a consultative conversation to a productized sale, providing clarity and a clear path forward.

The output of this step is a clear, confident recommendation. You will present the tier that represents the minimum requirement to solve the client's core problem, while framing the higher tiers as pathways to accelerate success, expand capability, or de-risk the project. This positions you as a trusted advisor guiding them to the right investment, not just a salesperson pushing the most expensive option.

## 3.0 Segment-Specific Pricing Strategies

While the core principles of value-based pricing are universal, their application must be precisely tailored to the unique business model, pains, and vocabulary of each target segment. The following guides provide the specific messaging, tier mapping, and objection handling required to win in each vertical.

### 3.1 Strategy for Hardware Vendors (OEMs)

**Value Proposition Summary:** We offer OEMs a battle-tested, white-label cloud OS that saves them over $20M and 18 months, allowing them to become "Intel Inside" and focus on hardware innovation.

#### Objection Handling

**Objection:** "We've already built 80% of our cloud."

**Value-Based Response:** 
> "Let's be candid: you're not 80% done with the valuable part. You're 20% into an infinite roadmap of enterprise requirements. The value isn't in the first 80%, which is table stakes for a basic dashboard. The real value—and the part that wins telco deals—is in the last 20%: the infinite roadmap. It's the pre-built integrations with Amdocs, the multi-vendor support that AT&T will demand, and the custom SSO that Cox requires. You're not buying software; you're buying the 8 years of battle-tested solutions that represent true, enterprise-grade capability."

---

### 3.2 Strategy for ISVs & Cloud Providers

**Value Proposition Summary:** We are the multi-vendor engine that allows ISVs to win 60% more RFPs by supporting legacy hardware in 90 days, not 2 years.

#### Objection Handling

**Objection:** "Why wouldn't our customers just go directly to you?"

**Value-Based Response:**
> "Because they don't buy infrastructure; they buy solutions. The value they see comes from your application layer—your carrier-grade features, your advanced analytics, and your vertical expertise. We operate like the Linux kernel; we're the powerful OS that makes your superior software even more valuable by allowing it to run on any hardware. The MsTECH win in Jamaica proved customers want both: your best-in-class features and our universal orchestration."

---

### 3.3 Strategy for Elite MSPs & Regional Telcos

**Value Proposition Summary:** We provide MSPs with the telco-grade capability to win the multi-vendor, multi-million dollar deals they are currently locked out of, turning a 5-person team into a national competitor.

#### Objection Handling

**Objection:** "We can build this ourselves for cheaper."

**Value-Based Response:**
> "The comparison isn't our $48k fee versus a developer's salary. It's about the total cost and the value of speed. The true cost to you is a minimum of **$1.5M and 18-24 months of development**. But here's the real cost: what is 18 months of lost opportunity worth? If you lose just two 500-location RFPs while you're building, that's $500K+ in lost annual revenue you'll never get back. The value you're buying isn't software; it's the ability to generate revenue from those large deals next quarter, not in 2026."

## 4.0 Positioning the Top Tier: The "Concierge" Offering

Our highest tiers—the OEM Partnership License, the ISV Integration License, and the United Edge Tier for MSPs—should never be positioned as mere products. They are **entry points into a strategic partnership**. This is our **"concierge" offering**, the answer to the question every ambitious client has: **"What would we do if money were no object?"**

This is where you use our most powerful proof points as anchors. The **$1.2M/year Ruckus deal** and the **$2M+/year billing from MsTECH's Jamaica contract** are not just case studies; they are benchmarks for the level of competition we enable.

Frame the United Edge tier's **$360k+ price point** as the **"entry fee to compete at that level."** You are not just buying software; you are securing the same elite engineering team that powered Ruckus's entry into the telco market for a fraction of what they paid. You are buying access to a class of business that was previously unattainable.

Ultimately, our entire sales motion is built on this philosophy. We sell transformative capability through a consultative process, using a structured pricing model that ensures clarity and scalability for our clients and for us. We identify their desired future state, quantify the immense value of reaching it, and present our tiers as the clearest, fastest, and most de-risked paths to get there. The final choice is theirs.

> **The Android moment is here. Either you're part of the unified OS, or you're the company still trying to build Symbian while the market moves on.**
