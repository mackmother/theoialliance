## Who They Are

**Julian Edwards, Head of Operations, MsTECH:**

> "We're a Jamaican IT consulting company—we design, implement, and manage network infrastructure for government and enterprise clients across the Caribbean. Before this project, we had about 30 people. The Ministry contract let us grow to 45."

**The gap:** MsTECH had deep expertise in switching, firewalls, and WAN infrastructure. But managed WiFi at national scale? That was new territory—and they were bidding against companies with 15-20 years of experience.

---

## The Challenge: Win It or Watch It Walk

The Jamaica Ministry of Education needed to connect 1,000 schools with reliable WiFi. It was the largest network infrastructure project in the country's history.

**The requirements that scared off most bidders:**

- **Multi-vendor mandatory.** Ruckus gear already deployed in some schools. Fortinet firewalls in place. The Ministry refused to rip-and-replace and refused to be locked into any single vendor.

- **90-day timeline.** Phase 1 required 364 schools live in 90 days. Four schools per day, including weekends, with Christmas in the middle.

- **Three-tier oversight.** Real-time dashboards for the Prime Minister's Office, management access for central IT, and individual views for school administrators—all with different permissions.

**Julian Edwards:**

> "We priced out the traditional approach. Meraki licensing for 7,000 APs would have been over a million dollars in year one—and it doesn't do multi-vendor. The alternative was building it ourselves: hire engineers, build integrations, 18 months and $600K before we'd deploy a single access point. The RFP required deployment in 90 days. The math didn't work."

**The stakes:** Pass on the contract and watch it go to an off-island competitor. MsTECH's biggest opportunity would become someone else's win.
