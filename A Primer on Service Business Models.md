# A Primer on Service Business Models
## Applying "Price the Client, Not the Job" to the WiBUZ MSP Strategy

---

## Introduction: Setting the Stage

This primer deconstructs the two fundamental service business models—**Customized** and **Productized**—using the powerful framework outlined by Blair Enns in his "Price the Client, Not the Job" methodology. By understanding the core principles that separate these approaches, we can gain critical insight into the market dynamics that govern service firms. The ultimate goal is to apply this understanding to analyze, refine, and sharpen the WiBUZ go-to-market and pricing strategy for our Managed Service Provider (MSP) partners.

---

## 1. The Two Business Models: Customized vs. Productized

To effectively price our service, we must first diagnose our own business model with clinical precision. In the world of expert firms, there are only two paths: **Customized** or **Productized**. Understanding which path we enable for our partners—and which we must walk ourselves—is the critical first step.

### 1.1. The Customized Service Firm: A Culture of Innovation

A **Customized Service Firm** is architected to solve unique, high-value problems for a select group of clients. It sells bespoke expertise, not a pre-packaged product. The sales process is consultative, involving deep conversations to uncover a client's specific challenges and desired outcomes. Its operations and culture are defined by the following key attributes:

- **Client Volume:** Typically serves between 8 and 12 active clients
- **Core Culture:** Driven by a culture of innovation and adding unique client value
- **Sales Model:** Employs a consultative, team-based selling approach, often involving senior principals
- **Key Characteristics:** Commands higher margins and fees but is inherently difficult to scale beyond a single office
- **Human Capital:** Relies on a team of expensive, "big brain" experts whose knowledge is the primary asset being sold

### 1.2. The Productized Service Firm: A Culture of Efficiency

A **Productized Service Firm** is built to deliver a standardized solution to a large volume of customers. It codifies its expertise into repeatable systems and processes, allowing it to serve a broader market efficiently. The sales process is more transactional, focused on fitting clients into predefined packages. Its operations and culture are defined by the following key attributes:

- **Client Volume:** Manages dozens or even hundreds of active clients
- **Core Culture:** Driven by a culture of efficiency and process optimization
- **Sales Model:** Utilizes a dedicated sales team executing a more transactional sales process
- **Key Characteristics:** Built for high volume and scale, but often faces greater commoditization and price competition
- **Human Capital:** Leverages a more structured HR model focused on training employees to execute well-defined systems

### 1.3. At a Glance: Comparing the Models

| Attribute | Customized Firm | Productized Firm |
|-----------|-----------------|------------------|
| Client Volume | Low (8-12 active clients) | High (Dozens to hundreds) |
| Primary Culture | Innovation | Efficiency |
| Sales Process | Consultative | Transactional |
| Scalability | Difficult to scale | Built to scale |

### 1.4. Section Transition

Having distinguished the two models, we now turn to the core pricing philosophy that allows a customized firm to capture the true worth of its services.

## 2. The Core Principle: Why You Must "Price the Client, Not the Job"

For customized firms, pricing based on inputs (time and materials) or outputs (deliverables) leaves immense value on the table. The key is to price based on the subjective value the client places on their desired outcome.

### 2.1. Uncovering Subjective Value

The foundational principle is that **all value is subjective**—it exists in the eye of the beholder. A service's worth isn't determined by the labor required to produce it, but by the impact it has on the client's business. This impact can be understood through the **Value Triad**:

1. **Revenue Gains:** Helping the client make more money (e.g., increase sales, improve profit)
2. **Cost Reductions:** Helping the client save money (e.g., reduce overhead, lower customer acquisition costs)
3. **Emotional Contributions:** The personal and psychological impact, such as delivering peace of mind, reducing risk, or making the client look good to their boss. These contributions act as powerful magnifiers of the economic value

### 2.2. A Tale of Two Clients: Why Context is Everything

To understand subjective value in practice, consider a simple request: **"generate as many high-quality leads as possible."**

**Client A:** Buff Whelen Chevrolet, a single car dealership  
**Client B:** The Chevrolet division of General Motors

The amount of work (inputs) and the percentage increase in sales (results) could be identical for both. However, the economic value of that result is orders of magnitude different. A 10% increase in leads for a local dealership might be worth thousands of dollars a month. For the national Chevrolet brand, that same percentage increase could be worth millions.

**This is the essence of value-based pricing: The work is irrelevant. The client's context is everything. Price the context, not the work.**

### 2.3. The Tool for Discovery: The Value Conversation

The method for uncovering this subjective value is the **Value Conversation**. It is a structured dialogue designed to move past inputs and outputs and focus on the economic and emotional worth of success. It revolves around three core questions:

1. **Desired Future State:** What does the future look like when we succeed? This question prompts the client to describe their ultimate goal, moving beyond tactical needs.

2. **Measurement:** How will we know we are there? This grounds the future state in concrete metrics and measurable outcomes.

3. **Worth:** If we achieve that, what would it be worth to you? This is the crucial question that quantifies the value of the solution and establishes the basis for pricing.

### 2.4. Section Transition

This pricing framework for high-value customized firms provides the exact lens we need to scrutinize our own productized strategy for MSPs.

## 3. Analysis: The WiBUZ MSP Strategy Through a New Lens

Applying this lens to our MSP partner strategy reveals a fascinating and intentional paradox at the heart of our business model.

### 3.1. Our Current Model: A Productized Approach

The WiBUZ offering for MSPs is structured into three distinct tiers, each with a fixed annual price and a defined set of capabilities:

- **Foundation:** $48,000 Annual Investment
- **Strategic:** $144,000 Annual Investment
- **United Edge:** $360,000+ Annual Investment

This tiered, package-based model with published price points is a classic example of a **Productized Service offering**. It is designed for efficiency and scale, allowing us to serve a broad base of MSP partners with a standardized, repeatable solution.

### 3.2. The Strategic Paradox: A Productized Platform for Customized Value

This paradox is not an accident or a flaw; it is the **cornerstone of our competitive advantage**. WiBUZ has engineered a productized, scalable platform whose sole purpose is to weaponize our MSP partners, transforming them into the high-margin, customized service providers that dominate the enterprise market.

Our platform is the engine that unlocks **"market access,"** allowing a 5-person MSP to win a national education contract in Jamaica by solving a complex multi-vendor integration challenge. That is not a productized, off-the-shelf problem; it is a unique, high-stakes challenge that required a consultative, customized approach to win.

**We sell a productized service that enables customized dominance.**

### 3.3. Justifying Our Approach: Why Productized is the Right Choice for WiBUZ

Our productized model is a conscious and correct strategic choice. To achieve our **"Android moment"** vision, we must be able to scale our platform and partner ecosystem efficiently. A purely customized, client-by-client model for WiBUZ itself would be impossible to scale.

Instead of viewing our tiers as rigid packages, we must frame them as a form of **value segmentation**. While our MSP partners must learn to **"price the client,"** WiBUZ must **"price the partner's ambition."** The tiers are not feature packages; they are investment levels aligned with the scale of the end-customer problems our partners want to solve:

- **Foundation:** Winning a $250K deal
- **Strategic:** Capturing regional enterprise contracts
- **United Edge:** Capturing a $2M+ national contract

### 3.4. Section Transition

Our model's architecture is sound. To maximize its potential, we must now integrate the best of both worlds into our go-to-market motion.

## 4. Recommendation: Fusing a Customized Sales Process with a Productized Model

Our product is already engineered for this reality. What must evolve is our commercial motion. We will not change our model; we will wrap it in a sales and marketing framework that mirrors the high-value consultative process we enable for our partners.

### 4.1. Adopting the Value Conversation in Our Sales Process

Our sales team must be trained to lead with the **Value Conversation**. The goal is not to create a unique price for every MSP. Instead, it is to deeply understand their business ambitions, the types of deals they are losing, and the economic value of the **"market access"** they currently lack. This diagnostic approach allows our team to:

1. Confidently place the MSP in the correct tier based on their strategic goals
2. Articulate the platform's ROI in terms that are specific and meaningful to that MSP's client-facing ambitions

### 4.2. Reframing Our Tiers from "Features" to "Unlocked Capabilities"

We must shift our messaging away from what our tiers include (inputs) and toward what they unlock (outcomes). This reframing aligns our productized packages with the customized value our partners want to deliver.

| Tier | Annual Investment | The "Price the Client" Value Proposition |
|------|------------------|------------------------------------------|
| **Foundation** | $48,000 | For MSPs ready to win their first multi-vendor deals worth over $250K, delivering a 5X+ return on investment from a single new contract. |
| **Strategic** | $144,000 | For MSPs scaling to compete with regional telcos on enterprise deals, unlocking telco-grade capability for a fraction of the cost. |
| **United Edge** | $360,000+ | For partners competing for national-scale contracts, providing the same elite engineering team that Ruckus paid $1.2M/year for, enabling wins like the $2M+/year Jamaica contract. |

### 4.3. Positioning LiveSDK as Our Customization Engine

Recall the core asset of the Customized Service Firm: the team of **"expensive, big brain" experts**. For our partners, **LiveSDK** is that team. We are externalizing the most difficult and costly component of a customized service model and providing it on-demand, enabling a 5-person MSP to wield the engineering prowess of a telco.

The allocated **"LiveSDK Engineering"** hours within each tier are the critical mechanism that bridges our productized platform and our partners' unique, customized needs. It is not a "support desk"; it is the **"elite engineering team"** that our partners deploy to solve the specific, deal-winning integration challenges that their end-customers present.

## 5. Conclusion: Our Strategic Path Forward

Our strategic path is clear and sound. The WiBUZ scalable, productized MSP model is the correct choice because its fundamental purpose is to **empower our partners to build their own customized, high-margin service businesses**. By fusing this powerful platform with a more consultative, value-first sales motion, we can accelerate our growth and create a truly dominant partner ecosystem.

### Key Actions Required

Our key actions must be:

1. **Train the sales team** on using the Value Conversation to diagnose MSP ambitions and quantify the value of unlocked market access.

2. **Reframe marketing and sales collateral** to position our tiers based on the "Unlocked Capabilities" and specific ROI they enable for our partners.

3. **Elevate the role of LiveSDK** in all messaging as our key differentiator and the engine for client-specific customization that wins telco-scale deals.

## Summary

This primer demonstrates that WiBUZ operates at the intersection of two business models: a productized platform that enables customized value delivery for MSP partners. By applying Blair Enns' "Price the Client, Not the Job" framework, we can refine our go-to-market strategy to emphasize the outcomes and market access our partners unlock, rather than the features our platform provides. This shift—from feature-based to value-based positioning—will strengthen our competitive advantage and accelerate partner adoption.
